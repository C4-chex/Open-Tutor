## Top 3 Marketing Highlights This Week

### 1. Show HN: Socratic FSM — 5-state machine that replaces a system prompt instruction
The October 20 community posts include a polished Show HN on the Socratic FSM. The central framing is sharp and repeatable: "Most AI tutors prepend 'use the Socratic method' to the prompt. We built a proper FSM instead." Five states (PROBE, CLARIFY, CONFRONT, SCAFFOLD, CONFIRM), 25 transitions, a classifier that emits 5 quality signals (correct/partial/wrong/confused/no_response), FSM state persisted per user+course in the agent KV store. The escape hatch — SCAFFOLD for 4+ turns without improvement flips to a direct worked example — is exactly the kind of concrete design decision Show HN commenters will probe. Initial-state selection from behavioral context (cognitive load, mastery score, error type) is a second differentiator beyond the FSM itself. The implementation is 287 lines in `apps/api/services/agent/socratic_engine.py` — auditable by any interested developer in under 10 minutes. **This is the strongest technical narrative in the October batch and should be the top posting priority.**

### 2. r/LocalLLaMA: Adaptive model routing by cognitive load signal
The October 20 r/LocalLLaMA post covers a genuinely useful pattern for local LLM practitioners: routing `"large"` vs. `"fast"` model hints based on real-time cognitive load rather than just task type. The mechanism is specific and verifiable — when cognitive load score > 0.7 (computed from 7 behavioral signals in 488 lines of `cognitive_load.py`), the Socratic engine shifts to SCAFFOLD state which requests the fast/3b model. The post includes concrete latency benchmarks (intent classification: 280–400ms on llama3.2:3b; full explanation on llama3.1:8b: 5–9s). This generalizes beyond OpenTutor to anyone building agentic loops on constrained hardware and should find a receptive audience on r/LocalLLaMA given their interest in multi-model local inference.

### 3. Week 42 Build-in-Public: 2-layer context compaction
The October 18 build-in-public thread covers context window management — a topic AI builders recognize but rarely write about carefully. The two-threshold approach (LLM-summarize at 70%, emergency drop at 90%), fixed summary structure (Goals/Progress/Decisions/Next Steps/Key Facts), intent-aware token budgets (a "learn" session allocates 4000 tokens to RAG docs vs. 1500 to history; a "review" session allocates 2500 to memory vs. 2000 to RAG), and incremental compaction that updates an existing summary rather than re-running from scratch — each is a concrete design choice that can anchor a Twitter thread beat. At 561 lines across two files, this is substantial enough to earn a "I spent N lines on something most apps ignore" framing that performs well on developer-audience Twitter.
